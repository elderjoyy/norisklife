Insurance explained in simple terms

Use these introductory guides to understand common concepts before exploring policy options.

Life Insurance Basics

Life insurance is designed to provide a financial benefit to the nominee or beneficiaries if the insured person dies during the period and under the conditions covered by the policy.

When learning about life insurance, it helps to understand these basic terms:

  • Life assured: the person whose life is covered by the policy.
  • Policyholder: the person who owns the policy and is responsible for its obligations.
  • Sum assured: the amount of cover specified in the policy, subject to its terms.
  • Policy term: the period for which the cover is intended to remain in force.
  • Premium: the amount payable to keep the policy active, as described in the contract.
  • Nominee: the person nominated to receive policy proceeds, subject to applicable law and policy conditions.

Before considering a policy, think about the people who depend on your income, outstanding liabilities, future family commitments and any existing financial resources or cover.

A calculator can help organise your assumptions, but it cannot determine the right policy or cover amount for every person. Review your circumstances and the complete policy wording.

Understanding Health Insurance

Health insurance may help meet eligible medical expenses in accordance with the policy. The extent of cover depends on the benefits, limits, exclusions and conditions in the specific contract.

Pay particular attention to the following:

  • Sum insured: the maximum cover available under the policy, subject to its terms and applicable limits.
  • Waiting periods: periods during which specified illnesses, treatments or conditions may not be covered.
  • Pre-existing conditions: conditions disclosed or assessed under the insurer's definitions and underwriting rules.
  • Exclusions: expenses, treatments or circumstances that the policy does not cover.
  • Room and treatment limits: restrictions or sub-limits that may apply to particular hospital expenses.
  • Cashless and reimbursement claims: different ways eligible claims may be settled, subject to the insurer's process and network arrangements.

Also check the policy's co-payment, deductible, restoration benefit, pre- and post-hospitalisation cover, renewal conditions and claim documentation requirements where applicable.

Do not assume that every hospital expense is covered or that every hospital is available for cashless treatment. Confirm current details with the insurer and read the policy wording.

Review Before You Decide

Insurance policies are contracts. Take time to understand what is covered, what is not covered and what you must do to keep the policy in force.

  • Read the policy brochure, benefit illustration where applicable, prospectus and full policy wording.
  • Check eligibility, age limits, premium payment frequency, policy duration and renewal provisions.
  • Understand exclusions, waiting periods, limits, deductibles and co-payments.
  • Provide complete and accurate information in the proposal form, including disclosures requested by the insurer.
  • Ask how claims are notified, assessed and supported by documents.
  • Keep copies of the proposal, policy schedule, receipts and communications.
  • Use the insurer's official channels to verify product details and seek clarification.

Ask questions until the important terms are clear. Do not rely only on a headline benefit, verbal summary or an estimate from an online tool.
